Tvoli Privacy Policy

Effective date: 17 October 2026

Tvoli is a TV remote app for iPhone and iPad. It sends commands from your device straight to TVs on your own Wi-Fi network. The Tvoli app does not collect your personal data, and we never sell or share it. We receive personal data only if you contact us (section 7). Tvoli has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and we run no server that receives information about you or about how you use the app.

This policy covers the Tvoli app, its widgets, Control Center controls, Live Activity and Siri/Shortcuts actions (together, "Tvoli"). It also serves as the information notice required by Article 13 of the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Article 10 of Türkiye's Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 (KVKK), and US state privacy laws, including the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended (CCPA).

2. Data the app collects

None. Tvoli sends nothing about you to us or to any third party, other than the commands and pairing data it sends to the TVs you choose on your local network (section 4). The data Tvoli needs to work stays on your iPhone or iPad and on your local network, under your control. We have no access to it.

Our App Store privacy label says "Data Not Collected" and "No tracking". Tvoli includes no third-party code: no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or attribution SDKs. It does not use the advertising identifier (IDFA) and does not ask for App Tracking Transparency permission.

Tvoli asks for one permission: Local Network access, so it can find and control TVs on your Wi-Fi. It does not use your camera, microphone, location, contacts, photos, Bluetooth or notifications.

3. What Tvoli stores on your device

To remember your TVs, Tvoli keeps this data on your device:

  • Your TVs: name, brand and platform, local IP address, model and, where the TV reports it, the TV's network (MAC) address. Tvoli uses the MAC address to try to wake the TV (Wake-on-LAN), which may not work on every TV or network.
  • Your selected TV and the scenes you create (name, icon, target TV and steps).
  • Pairing credentials that some TVs issue or require: Samsung and Vizio tokens, the LG client key, Philips login credentials, the Sony pre-shared key you enter, a client certificate with its private key that Tvoli generates on your device to pair with Android TV and Google TV, and a flag that records whether an Android TV is paired.
  • A random Vizio client ID that Tvoli creates once per installation and sends to Vizio TVs.

Where the data lives:

  • Tvoli stores the TV list, scenes and pairing credentials in the iOS Keychain with the "this device only" setting. iOS does not sync these items through iCloud Keychain or move them to another device. They can appear in an encrypted backup of the same device but can only be restored to that device.
  • Tvoli stores the Vizio client ID in the app's standard settings storage, outside the Keychain. If you installed an early TestFlight test build, your TV list from that build may also remain there. This storage is part of your normal iCloud or computer backups. Apple or you hold those backups. We have no access to them.

How to delete it. Removing a TV in Tvoli takes it off your list. In this version it does not delete that TV's pairing credentials or any scenes that point to it. iOS also keeps Keychain items after you delete an app, so your TVs and pairings return if you reinstall Tvoli on the same device. To remove all of it, erase the device in Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone (or iPad) > Erase All Content and Settings. You can also revoke Tvoli's access on the TV itself, in the TV's connected-devices or mobile-app settings.

Logs. Tvoli writes short technical messages (TV brand, button name, error text) to the device's system log for troubleshooting. iOS hides the TV's address in these logs by default. The log stays on your device. We do not receive it.

4. What Tvoli sends to your TV

Tvoli talks to your TVs directly over your local network. This traffic does not pass through us or any server we run. To find TVs, Tvoli uses Bonjour and checks devices on your local network segment for the ports that TV control services use. It reads each TV's name, model and, where the TV reports it (for example Samsung, Roku and Vizio), its network (MAC) address. Discovery contacts devices on your local network segment for a few seconds. Use Tvoli only on networks you own or are allowed to use.

To control a TV, Tvoli sends:

  • the button commands you press;
  • text you type on the keyboard screen, which goes only to the selected TV and is not stored;
  • the app name "Tvoli" and the technical identifiers that the TV's pairing process requires.

Some TVs list Tvoli under a generic or compatible-app name. For example, Philips TVs may show it as an Android device. This is the identifier those TVs accept for pairing. It does not mean Tvoli is made or approved by the TV maker.

Wake-on-LAN sends your TV's MAC address as a broadcast on your local network, so other devices on that network can see it. iOS may block this broadcast, so Wake-on-LAN may not work.

Your TV is made and run by its manufacturer. What the TV does with the commands and data it receives, including anything it sends to its maker, follows the manufacturer's own privacy policy, not this one.

5. Purchases

Apple sells Tvoli subscriptions and the lifetime purchase through the App Store and processes all payments. We receive no payment details, name or e-mail address from Apple. Tvoli checks your purchase status on your device through Apple's StoreKit each time it needs to. Apple's privacy policy applies to your purchase: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/.

Apple gives developers sales reports and, if you allow it in your device settings, aggregated app analytics and crash reports. These reports do not identify you to us.

6. Apple features: widgets, Live Activity, Siri and Shortcuts

Tvoli's widgets, Control Center controls and Live Activity run on your device. The Live Activity shows the name of your selected TV on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, so anyone who can see your screen can see it. Tvoli does not use push notifications to update it.

If you use Siri, Apple processes your voice request under Apple's privacy policy. Tvoli receives only the resulting action, such as "volume up", and runs it on your device.

9. Sharing and transfers abroad

We do not sell or rent personal data or disclose it for advertising. Only the service provider that hosts our e-mail or support channel stores your messages, and only so we can answer you. We may also disclose a message you sent us if the law requires it, for example under a court order.

When you write to us, you send your message to a developer in Türkiye, outside the EEA and UK. The provider that carries and stores it may keep it on servers in other countries, for example the United States. That provider's own transfer safeguards apply to the data it holds. We have not signed separate standard contractual clauses with it.

Under KVKK, this transfer abroad happens only occasionally, when you choose to write to us. We rely on the exceptions for occasional transfers in Article 9(6) of KVKK: the transfer is needed to handle the request you started with us, or you give your explicit consent by sending your message through that channel. We do not use this route for regular or routine transfers. You can ask us for details at support@melyro.app.

Data that Tvoli keeps on your device is not transferred anywhere by us.

10. Retention

We delete support messages 12 months after the conversation ends, unless we need them for a legal claim. In that case we keep only the relevant messages until the claim is settled. Data on your device stays there until you delete it (section 3).

11. Security

Tvoli keeps pairing credentials in the iOS Keychain, readable only after you first unlock the device after a restart and never synced to other devices. Tvoli uses encrypted connections where the TV supports them. It accepts the TV's self-signed certificate only for addresses on your local network.

Some TVs accept only unencrypted connections on the local network, including Roku, Sony, older Philips, older Samsung (port 8001) and some LG models. On those connections, others on your Wi-Fi network could see the commands, the text you type and, for Sony and Samsung, the key or token used to pair. Use Tvoli only on a network you trust.
